Fit & tailoring · 5 min read

What happens at a bespoke fitting?

A first fitting is a conversation as much as a measurement. Here’s how the process turns your preferences into a garment that moves with you.

Start with how you wear it

We begin with the occasions, climate, and routines the garment should serve. That context helps guide cloth weight, construction, and the details that matter to you.

Measurements are only the beginning

Shoulder balance, posture, and movement all affect how a jacket sits. Your tailor records measurements and discusses your preferred ease and silhouette before pattern work begins.

Refine through fittings

At each try-on, we check balance, comfort, and proportion. Adjustments are reviewed with you so the final garment feels intentional rather than simply close to a standard size.

Bring a shirt and shoes similar to the ones you plan to wear with the suit. It helps the fitting team assess the full silhouette.

Cloth · 4 min read

Choosing cloth for a first suit.

The right cloth balances how a suit feels, where you wear it, and how often it earns a place in your rotation.

Think about use before color

Consider the settings where you’ll wear the suit most. A versatile mid-weight cloth can travel between work and events, while a lighter open weave may suit warmer climates.

Understand weight and weave

Weight influences warmth and drape. Weave affects texture, breathability, and how formal the cloth appears. Your tailor can show swatches in daylight and help compare them against your wardrobe.

Choose what you’ll wear

Navy, charcoal, and restrained patterns are dependable first choices, but comfort and personal taste matter most. Ask to handle the swatches and see how they pair with your usual shirts and shoes.

Care · 3 min read

Keep a tailored jacket in shape.

A few quiet care habits help a tailored jacket recover between wears and stay ready for the next occasion.

Let the cloth rest

Rotate jackets rather than wearing the same piece every day. After a full day, hang the garment on a broad, shaped hanger and let it air before putting it away.

Brush, don’t over-clean

A soft clothes brush removes surface dust. Spot cleaning and frequent pressing can be hard on natural fibers, so use professional cleaning only when needed and tell the cleaner about the garment’s construction.

Store with room to breathe

Keep jackets in a cool, dry space with enough room to hang naturally. Use a breathable garment cover for longer storage and avoid compressing the shoulders.

Occasion · 4 min read

Planning wedding tailoring backwards.

A clear timeline leaves enough room for fabric selection, fittings, and finishing before the celebration.

Begin with the event date

Book a consultation several weeks ahead. Bespoke garments usually need time for cloth sourcing, pattern work, construction, and fittings; seasonal demand can affect availability.

Allow time for a fitting

Plan at least one fitting before the final week. If coordinating a wedding party, share the schedule early so each person can be measured and fitted without a last-minute rush.

Confirm the whole look

Bring details about the dress code, venue, and season. The studio can help coordinate cloth, shirt, and finishing choices. Final timing and price are confirmed during consultation.
